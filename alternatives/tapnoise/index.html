TapNoise alternative with a free trial and a $3.99 price

TapNoise is a playful Mac app with 20 sounds and a desktop companion. Its regular price is $14.99 for one Mac, and it has no trial. Keyslam is $3.99 once for all your Macs, with 7 days to try it.

Short answer: Keyslam is a TapNoise alternative for people who want sounds cut from real keyboard recordings and a chance to try it first. It has a 7-day free trial (TapNoise removed its trial in version 0.2.5 on October 11, 2026), costs $3.99 once for all the Macs you own (TapNoise's regular prices are $14.99 for 1 Mac and $20.99 for 2), runs on macOS 13 Ventura (TapNoise needs macOS 14), loads your own sound packs, and plays real strikes from named CC0 recordings. How each app reacts to how you type

TapNoise changes volume with an estimate of your typing movement. Its pressure features need a MacBook with the motion sensor, much like Keyslam's force sensing: “MacBook Pro with M1 Pro or M1 Max, and later MacBook Air with M2, and later” (system requirements (archived)). Its own blog is upfront about the method: “It is an estimate from body movement, not a force sensor measuring each individual switch.” (TapNoise blog (archived))

Keyslam reads the MacBook accelerometer about 800 times a second and compares each keystroke's jolt with how you usually type. It then picks and blends real strikes from four strength bands. Each pack's strikes are cut from one real typing recording and sorted by natural loudness into soft (about the 8th to 30th percentile), medium (38th to 62nd), hard (70th to 88th) and slam (the hardest ~8%), and each band is leveled to a consistent target. So a hard press plays a different real strike, not a louder copy of a soft one. Force sensing in detail.

Neither approach is a per-key force sensor; a MacBook doesn't have one. On a Mac without the motion sensor, Keyslam plays every sound at a strength you pick.

Where the sounds come from

TapNoise has 20 sounds, “10 keyboards and 10 fun sounds.” Its notices page says five of the themes, plus its slap and MacBook sounds, came from an AI audio generator: “Thock, Creamy, Clicky, Typewriter and Bubble, plus the shared slap and MacBook sounds: the lid chimes, hinge creak, and charger and USB sounds. The source files were generated with ElevenLabs.” And: “8-bit and Laser are synthesised by TapNoise.” (TapNoise notices (archived))

Other differences

  • Notarization: “TapNoise is signed by its maker but not notarized by Apple.” (TapNoise help (archived)), so the first launch needs Open Anyway. Keyslam's public download is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple.
  • Your own sounds: TapNoise's comparison table answers “No” to “Your own sound packs” (TapNoise compare (archived)). Keyslam loads a folder of WAV, AIFF or FLAC files with a small pack.json from its Packs folder.
  • Network: “It has no account, no analytics and no advertising. It goes online only to check your license with Dodo Payments, and once a day to look for an update.” (TapNoise privacy (archived)) Keyslam makes no network connections; its license is checked offline.
  • macOS: TapNoise needs macOS 14 Sonoma (system requirements (archived)); Keyslam needs macOS 13 Ventura. Both run on Apple silicon and Intel. Which Macs stop at Ventura.
  • Checkout: “Dodo Payments sells the license as merchant of record” (TapNoise notices (archived)). Keyslam sells through Stripe Checkout; the seller is Venture Deals, Co.

Pick TapNoise if

  • You want a desktop companion. “Tappy, a little keycap on your desktop” (tapnoise.com (archived))
  • You code with AI agents. Its pricing page lists “Agent Sounds for Claude Code, Codex and Cursor” (pricing (archived)).
  • You want fun sounds as well as keyboards. Half of its 20 sounds aren't keyboards, and it has slap, lid-chime, hinge, charger and USB sounds.

Pick Keyslam if you'd like to try before you pay, want to spend $3.99 instead of $14.99, use more than two Macs, want your own packs, or are on macOS 13.

TapNoise questions

Does TapNoise have a free trial?

No. Its site says it “needs a license key from the first launch”. Version 0.2.4 added a 3-day trial on October 10, 2026, and 0.2.5 removed it the next day. You can still play its sound demos in the browser, and it refunds within 7 days.

How much will TapNoise cost after launch pricing ends?

$14.99 for Solo (1 Mac) or $20.99 for Duo (2 Macs), one time, plus tax. Launch prices end after the first 1,000 licenses or 14 days after launch day, whichever comes first; its launch counter showed November 15, 2026 when we checked on November 4, 2026.

Is TapNoise notarized?

No. Its help page says it is “signed by its maker but not notarized by Apple”, so macOS blocks the first launch until you allow it with Open Anyway.

Can I add my own sounds to TapNoise?

Not according to its own comparison table, which answers “No” for “Your own sound packs”. Keyslam loads custom packs: a folder of WAV, AIFF or FLAC files with a pack.json.
