How to make your MacBook sound like a mechanical keyboard

MacBook keys are built to be quiet. A keyboard sound app plays a recording of a real mechanical board each time a key goes down, in whatever app you type in. Here's the full setup, the free options, and the parts no app can change.

How do I add keyboard sounds to a Mac?

macOS can't do it on its own, so you add an app that listens for key presses and plays a sound for each one. It needs one permission, Input Monitoring, because it has to notice keys you press in other apps. The sound comes out of your speakers or headphones. The keyboard itself doesn't change.

Step by step: set up Keyslam on a MacBook

  1. Get the app. Download Keyslam, our keyboard sound app for Mac, as a notarized .dmg for Apple silicon and Intel. Open it and drag Keyslam to your Applications folder.
  2. Open it. There's no window and no Dock icon. A keyboard icon appears in the menu bar at the top of the screen. Click it to open the menu.
  3. Allow Input Monitoring. The menu shows an orange card: “Keyslam needs Input Monitoring”. Click Open Settings. It opens System Settings › Privacy & Security › Input Monitoring, where you turn Keyslam on. If macOS wants the app restarted, a Relaunch Keyslam button appears in the same card.
  4. Pick a keyboard. The Sound grid lists all 15 boards. Click one and it plays a short phrase right away. Preview plays it again. Every pack is leveled to the same loudness (−28 LUFS), so one board isn't suddenly louder than another.
  5. Set strength and volume. On a MacBook with the motion sensor, Feel how hard you type is on. A four-part meter lights up soft, medium, hard or slam as you type. Slide Sensitivity right to reach hard and slam with less force, or left to save them for real hits. On other Macs, or with force sensing off, choose Soft, Medium, Hard or Slam as a fixed strength. Then set the volume slider.
  6. Fine-tune it. The checkboxes at the bottom of the menu finish the job, among them Key release sounds (the key coming back up), Stereo by key position (Q a little left, P a little right) and Open at login, so the sound is there after a restart.

How do I change the keyboard sound on a MacBook Air or Pro?

You can't change the sound the built-in keys make, because macOS has no setting for it. What you can change is the sound played with each key. In Keyslam, click the menu bar icon and pick a different board in the Sound grid. The change is instant, and it works the same on MacBook Air and MacBook Pro.

Can a laptop keyboard sound mechanical?

The sound, yes. The feel, no. An app can't add key travel, a heavier spring or a tactile bump. What it can do is make each keystroke sound like a real board, and the details decide whether it convinces you or sounds like a loop.

Each Keyslam pack is cut from a real typing recording. Every strike is detected, then the strikes are sorted by natural loudness into four bands, and each band is leveled to a consistent target. On the keyboard packs each strike is paired with its key-up, and Space, Return and Delete get their own takes; the two typewriters play a separately recorded bell on Return. Every key keeps its own take and pitch, and stereo placement follows the key's position.

Does macOS have built-in typing sounds?

Not for everyday typing. The only built-in key click is part of Slow Keys, an accessibility feature that also makes every key wait before it types. Where Mac typing sounds come from, and how to turn them on or off.

Caveats before you start

  • The feel stays the same. Only the sound changes. Travel and resistance are still MacBook keys.
  • Bluetooth adds delay. Keyslam starts the sound as the key goes down, with an audio buffer of about 5 ms. Bluetooth headphones add their own delay on top, so wired headphones or the built-in speakers feel tightest.
  • Force sensing needs the sensor. The M1 MacBook Air, the 13-inch M1 MacBook Pro, Intel Macs and desktops play at the strength you pick. Check which MacBooks have an accelerometer.
  • External keyboards work. The sensor feels the MacBook's case, so keys on an external board play at your usual strength.
  • Password fields stay silent. macOS doesn't deliver those keystrokes to any app.
  • Calls. On speakers, your microphone can pick the sound up. Wear headphones, or flip the switch at the top of Keyslam's menu off before the call.
  • Battery. After 3 minutes without typing, the audio and the motion sensor switch off.

Questions about MacBook keyboard sounds

Do I need to keep the app running?

Yes. The sound comes from the app, so it stops when you quit. Keyslam lives in the menu bar with no Dock icon, and the Open at login checkbox starts it after every restart.

Can I make the MacBook's own keys louder or clickier?

No. MacBook keys are a low-travel design and macOS has no setting that changes how they sound. You can add a sound with an app, or plug in a real mechanical keyboard over USB or Bluetooth.

Should I buy a mechanical keyboard instead?

If you want the feel as well as the sound, yes. Only a real board changes how the keys move. A sound app costs less, adds nothing to your bag and works on the MacBook keyboard in your lap. You can also use both: Keyslam plays for external keyboards too.
