Opera GX keyboard sounds on Mac, inside and outside the browser

Opera GX can play a sound for each key you type, and Opera GX runs on a Mac. The sounds stop at the edge of the browser window, though. Here's how to set it up, why it stops, and what to use when you want sounds everywhere.

Updated November 2, 2026

Short answer: Opera GX's keyboard sounds are a browser feature, set under Settings › Mods › Keyboard Sounds. On a Mac they play while you type in Opera GX and stop when you switch to another app. Typing-sound extensions for Chrome work the same way. For sounds in Pages, Slack, Terminal and the rest of macOS, use a Mac app with Input Monitoring permission, such as Keyslam.

What Opera GX's keyboard sounds are

Opera describes them as built into GX. There are preloaded sounds under Settings › Mods, more packs in the GX Store, and an option to import your own audio files and adjust volume, intensity and effects (Opera's keyboard sounds page, checked November 2, 2026). Opera GX runs on macOS; Opera's download page lists a Universal installer.

Turn them on, change them or turn them off on a Mac

  1. Open Opera GX's settings. Press Command-comma (⌘,) in Opera GX.
  2. Go to Mods, then Keyboard Sounds. Opera keeps its preloaded sounds there.
  3. Pick a sound and set the volume. Choose a preloaded sound or a pack from the GX Store. Opera says you can also import your own audio files and adjust volume, intensity and effects.
  4. To turn them off, come back here. Settings › Mods › Keyboard Sounds, then disable or mute them.

Why they go quiet outside the browser

On a Mac, an app gets your key presses only while one of its windows is in front. The main exception is an app you've allowed in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Input Monitoring, which can notice keys you press in other apps. Opera's page describes no system-wide mode for GX keyboard sounds, so they follow the browser: switch to Notes or Xcode and they stop.

Extensions are narrower still. They run inside web pages, so they can't hear keys in other apps, and they usually stay silent in the browser's own address bar and settings pages too. Extensions you'll find by searching for “keyboard sounds Chrome extension” or “typing sounds extension” work this way: sounds for your tabs, not the rest of the Mac.

Permissions and privacy, side by side

Extensions. An extension that plays a sound on any site has to run on that site. Chrome shows this on the extension's Details page as “Allow this extension to read and change all your data on websites you visit”, and lets you limit it to specific sites (Google's instructions). That reaches page content, not just key presses.

Opera GX. Its sounds come with the browser, so there's no separate permission to grant. They know about keys typed in Opera GX, which the browser receives anyway.

A Mac app. Keyslam needs Input Monitoring. That covers more places (any app) but less content: it lets an app see key presses, not what's on the page or in your files. Keyslam stores nothing you type and makes no network connections, and its license is verified offline. More on whether Input Monitoring is safe.

The short version: install Keyslam, allow Input Monitoring, pick a board. Every step is in how to make your MacBook sound like a mechanical keyboard. Then turn off GX's own sounds at Settings › Mods › Keyboard Sounds, or you'll hear two sounds for each key while you're in the browser.

Which should you use?

  • Stay with Opera GX's sounds if you mostly type in the browser, want it free, or like GX Store packs and your own audio files.
  • Use an extension if you're on Chrome and only want sounds on a few sites. Limit its site access to those sites.
  • Use a Mac app if you want the sound in your editor, chat app and terminal too, recordings of named keyboards, or a sound that follows how hard you type on a MacBook.

Questions about browser keyboard sounds

Do Opera GX keyboard sounds work outside the browser?

No. They play while you type in Opera GX and stop when another app is in front. For sounds in other Mac apps you need a keyboard sound app with Input Monitoring permission.

Is a keyboard sound extension safe?

It can be, but check its site access first. To play on any site it has to run on that site, and Chrome describes that as permission to read and change your data on websites you visit. Limit it to specific sites on its Details page, and remove extensions you no longer use.

Do Opera GX keyboard sounds work on a Mac?

Opera GX runs on macOS (Opera's download page lists a Universal installer), and Opera describes keyboard sounds as built into GX, under Settings › Mods, without naming a platform. Either way, they play only inside the browser.
