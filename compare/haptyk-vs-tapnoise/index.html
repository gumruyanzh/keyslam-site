How each one reacts to a hard press

Haptyk and Keyslam read the same sensor. Newer Apple silicon MacBooks have an accelerometer under the keyboard, and both apps use each keystroke's jolt to decide how loud and how hard the strike sounds. Haptyk describes four levels, soft to slam. Keyslam sorts each board's real strikes by natural loudness into four bands and plays a different strike for a hard press, not a louder copy of a soft one. How Keyslam's force sensing works.

TapNoise estimates it. Its pricing page lists “Harder press, louder sound”, and its blog is upfront about the method: “It is an estimate from body movement, not a force sensor measuring each individual switch.” (TapNoise blog (archived)) That effect needs a sensor MacBook too.

The difference that matters most for buying: Haptyk won't run at all on a Mac without the sensor, while TapNoise and Keyslam play their sounds on any supported Mac.

Which one to pick

  • Pick Haptyk if you have a sensor MacBook, want 16 packs, or already like the Mechvibes and ClickClack switch sounds.
  • Pick TapNoise if you want its extras: “Tappy, a little keycap on your desktop” (tapnoise.com (archived)), “Agent Sounds for Claude Code, Codex and Cursor” (pricing (archived)), and fun sounds such as slaps, lid chimes and charger clicks, and you use one or two Macs.
  • Pick Keyslam if you use more than one Mac, a desktop Mac, an Intel Mac or macOS 13; if you'd like 7 days to try it before paying; if you want an app that never goes online; or if you'd rather pay $3.99 than $8 or $14.99.

Haptyk vs TapNoise questions

Haptyk or TapNoise: which is better?

It depends on your Mac. Haptyk ($8) reads the MacBook accelerometer and has 16 packs, but runs only on Apple silicon MacBooks with the motion sensor. TapNoise ($14.99 for one Mac) runs on any Mac with macOS 14, estimates typing pressure from movement, and adds Tappy and coding-agent sounds, but has no trial. Keyslam reads the same sensor as Haptyk for $3.99, also runs on Macs without it, and has a 7-day trial.

Does TapNoise charge per Mac?

Yes. Solo covers 1 Mac for $14.99 and Duo covers 2 for $20.99, one time, plus tax. It lists no plan for 3 or more Macs, so three Macs means buying Duo and Solo ($35.98). A Keyslam license covers all the Macs you own for $3.99.

How many Macs can I use Haptyk on?

Its terms say up to 3 devices you own. Each has to be an Apple silicon MacBook with the motion sensor, since Haptyk doesn't run on Intel Macs, desktop Macs, the M1 MacBook Air or the 13-inch M1 MacBook Pro.

Is Haptyk more expensive than Keyslam?

Yes: $8 against $3.99, about twice as much. Both are one-time purchases with free updates. Haptyk's license covers up to 3 devices; Keyslam's covers all the Macs you own.

Can I use one Keyslam license on my MacBook and my iMac?

Yes. It's a personal license for all the Macs you own, and nothing counts activations. The MacBook gets force sensing if it has the motion sensor; the iMac plays every sound at a strength you pick.
