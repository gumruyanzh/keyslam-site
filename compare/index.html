Which Mac keyboard sound apps react to how hard you type?

Several Mac apps react to typing force: Haptyk ($8, MacBooks with the sensor only), Keyslam ($3.99) and Clank Keys (free) read the MacBook accelerometer, and TapNoise varies volume from an estimate of typing movement. Thock (App Store) calls its packs “velocity aware” without saying how. The other 13 apps in this comparison don't advertise anything like it.

Haptyk's home page still presents it as the one app where “the sound follows how hard you press”1. Clank Keys does the same for free, and has since September 13, 2026, when its first public build came out2.

Two caveats apply to the three apps that read the sensor. Force sensing needs a MacBook with Apple's motion sensor: MacBook Pro with M1 Pro or M1 Max and later, and MacBook Air and MacBook Pro with M2 and later. And Haptyk's refund page says the sensor interface “is not officially supported by Apple and may change or become unavailable in future macOS updates”10. Keyslam reads the same sensor through IOKit, so that caution applies to it too. How Keyslam turns a jolt into a band is on the force sensing page.

Will it run on my Mac?

Two things decide it: whether your Mac has the motion sensor, and which macOS it runs. “Apple silicon, no sensor” means the M1 MacBook Air, the 13-inch M1 MacBook Pro, and every iMac, Mac mini, Mac Studio and Mac Pro with Apple silicon. Comparison questions

What is the best keyboard sound app for Mac?

It depends on your Mac. For sound that follows how hard you type: Keyslam ($3.99; macOS 13 or later, and it also runs on Macs without the sensor) or Haptyk ($8, MacBooks with the motion sensor only); Clank Keys does it free on Apple silicon MacBooks with the sensor, but isn't notarized. To buy on the Mac App Store: Klack ($4.99, macOS 15 or later). Free and open source: Mechvibes, Thock (open source) or Keyboard Sounds Pro, and KeyEcho is free with paid packs. We make Keyslam, so check the sources on this page.

Are Klack and Klakk the same app?

No. Klakk's own site says “Klakk (K-l-a-k-k) and Klack (K-l-a-c-k) are separate apps from different developers.” Klack is Henrik Ruscon's $4.99 Mac App Store app. Klakk sells $4.49 Mac and Windows editions on tryklakk.com, with a 3-day trial, and a free download with paid upgrades on the App Store.

Why isn't Keyslam on the Mac App Store?

Keyslam is sold directly from its own website, not through the Mac App Store. We haven't tested whether Apple would approve an App Store version that reads the MacBook motion sensor, so we don't claim it's impossible. Which keyboard sound apps send data from my Mac?

Going by their own pages: Haptyk sends usage statistics at launch, every 5 minutes and at quit. Mechvibes sends one anonymous ping per launch (you can switch it off) and checks GitHub daily. Klakk has activation and usage telemetry. TapNoise goes online to check its license and once a day for updates, and Keeby's direct edition checks its license periodically. Clank Keys says it has no networking code, TypeLouder says it works entirely offline, and Keyslam makes no network connections.

How do you keep this comparison accurate?

We quote each maker's own page, save an archive.org copy where archive.org can make one, and recheck monthly: TapNoise after its launch pricing ends on November 15, 2026, Haptyk after each release. Every change goes in the corrections log on this page. If you spot an error, tell us.
