Keyslam questions, answered straight

What Keyslam does today, what it doesn't do yet, and the details behind each answer. Every “no” here is a real no, checked against the app.

Download for MacTry it in your browser →

Updated November 2, 2026

Short answer: Keyslam is a Mac-only menu-bar app (macOS 13 Ventura or later, Apple silicon or Intel) that plays real keyboard recordings as you type. It works with any keyboard and with AirPods, needs no account or internet, and costs $3.99 once after a 7-day trial. It doesn't play mouse clicks. ⌃⌥⌘K turns the sounds off or on from any app, and they pause on their own while an app is using the microphone.

The questions people ask most

Does Keyslam go quiet during video calls?

Yes, by default. While any app is using the microphone, as on a video call, Keyslam pauses its sounds and the menu says “Paused while the mic is in use”; they come back when the mic is free. Keyslam doesn't record or open the microphone and asks for no microphone permission; it only checks whether the input is in use. On macOS 13 with AirPods or a USB headset as the microphone it may not notice the call, so press the on/off shortcut instead. Untick Quiet during calls in the menu to keep sounds playing.

Is there a shortcut to switch the sounds off?

Yes. Press ⌃⌥⌘K (Control-Option-Command-K) in any app to turn the sounds off or back on. Next to “Turn sounds on/off with” in the menu you can pick ⌃⌥⌘S, ⌃⌥⌘M or ⌃⌥⌘T instead, or switch the shortcut off. It needs no extra permission, and if another app already uses the combination, the menu says so.

Can Keyslam play louder than my Mac's volume?

No. Keyslam's volume slider starts at about half and goes to about twice that level at the right end, but it sits under the Mac's own volume. If it's still quiet, turn the Mac up.

Does Keyslam play mouse click sounds?

No. Keyslam listens to the keyboard only, so clicks and trackpad taps stay silent. Letters, numbers, Space, Return, Delete, the modifier keys and Caps Lock all play, and most keys add a softer key-up sound when you let go (the two typewriters don't).

Can I use Keyslam on Windows, an iPhone or a Chromebook?

No. Keyslam is a Mac app for macOS 13 Ventura or later. On a Mac it plays while you type in any app, Chrome included. To hear the boards on any other device, use the free keyboard simulator in a browser.
