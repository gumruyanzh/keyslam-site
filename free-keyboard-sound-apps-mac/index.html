What “free” means with each app

Mechvibes 3

Mechvibes is free and MIT-licensed, and its library is free too: 985 community pack pages on mechvibes.com when we counted, plus an editor for making your own. Version 3.4.0 of Mechvibes 3, which is written in Rust, came out on November 1, 2026. On the Mac, the project is upfront about the rough edges: its macOS README says the build “has never been run on a real Mac by the maintainer”. One-click pack install works on Windows and Linux; on a Mac you download a pack's ZIP and import it. It sends one anonymous Aptabase ping per launch, which you can turn off, and checks GitHub for updates once a day. The Homebrew cask has been disabled since September 1, 2026, and Mechvibes 2 is no longer developed. Can't get it to play? See why Mechvibes isn't working on a Mac.

KeyEcho

KeyEcho, from WaveApp LLC, is open source under AGPL-3.0, with notarized Mac builds and a Homebrew cask. You get 18 packs free. Each of the other 53 is a $3.99 Crafted Pack, or $9.99 buys “All Sounds Forever”. There's no trial for those, but there is a 14-day refund with no reason required, and one key works on Windows, macOS and Linux. Every key has its own press and release sample, and the app sends no usage analytics or crash reports. How Keyslam compares with KeyEcho.

Thock (open source)

Kamil Lobinski's Thock is the free one: MIT-licensed, macOS 13.5 or later, installed with Homebrew or a ZIP from GitHub. It loads custom packs written in simple JSON, has an in-app registry of community packs, mutes itself while music plays, places each sound by key position, and comes with a Raycast extension and a command-line tool. The latest release when we checked was 1.23.0, from March 28, 2026. Its site is clear about the name: this Thock “is not distributed on the App Store and is not affiliated with any app that has taken this name”. The $4.99 Thock on the Mac App Store is a different app; the two Thock apps, side by side.

Keyboard Sounds Pro

Keyboard Sounds Pro, by Nathan Fiscaletti, is free and MIT-licensed. It ships 20 built-in profiles (13 keyboard, 7 mouse), lets you build your own, and has pitch shift, pan and equalizer effects. Its website title still reads “Customizable Keyboard & Mouse Sounds for Windows & Linux”, but desktop v0.3.2, released May 1, 2026, includes a universal macOS DMG, built for both Apple silicon and Intel Macs. The pages we checked don't say whether the Mac build is notarized or what, if anything, it sends online.

Clank Keys

Of the free apps we found, Clank Keys is the one with force-sensitive sound, on MacBooks with the motion sensor. Its README says it “Reads the accelerometer hidden inside your MacBook and plays a real switch recording that matches how hard you hit the key.” Without the sensor, it varies the sound by typing rhythm. The switch recordings come from the Mechvibes project (MIT). The trade-offs are in setup: the source isn't published (the GitHub repo holds release builds only), the app isn't notarized, and you clear macOS's quarantine flag with a Terminal command before the first launch. The README also says the app contains no networking code. It needs macOS 13 and Apple silicon. Version 1.3.0, its first public build, came out on September 13, 2026.

Free on the Mac App Store: Tacque, KeyClicker and Klakk

Tacque, by Luminoxe, is free with an optional $1.99 tip, needs macOS 15, and plays six synthesized packs with spatial sound and per-key pitch variation. Its Setapp listing also mentions importing custom sounds and pausing during calls and media. KeyClicker, by Chujiang Wang, is free with a $1.99 All Access Lifetime purchase, needs macOS 14, and adds typewriter sounds and an on-screen display of the shortcuts you press. Both have “Data Not Collected” privacy labels. Klakk is a free App Store download with $4.99 and $2.99 Full Version purchases; its website editions cost $4.49 after a 3-day trial. Klakk isn't Klack: they come from different developers.

Kachunk's free tier

Kachunk, from Trifecta Labs, is “free to download and keep forever” with two packs: the Underwood 1938 typewriter and the Cream mechanical pack. $4.99 once unlocks all 26, including an Olivetti typewriter, on every Mac you own, with a 30-day refund. Its site says it's notarized, works offline and has zero telemetry. For a free typewriter sound, start here.

Is there a free Klack alternative?

Klack itself isn't free. It's $4.99 on the Mac App Store with no trial; its FAQ suggests previewing each switch on its website, and it offers a 14-day refund (App Store refunds go through Apple). The nearest free match on paper is Tacque: also on the Mac App Store, also spatial sound, and the same macOS 15 minimum. The difference is the sound itself: Tacque's packs are synthesized, while Klack says each of its switch sets is made of over 100 audio files, each recorded and mastered individually. KeyClicker and Kachunk are free App Store downloads with paid upgrades. Outside the App Store, Mechvibes, KeyEcho and the open-source Thock are free. Weighing paid options too? When free is enough, and when $3.99 is worth it

Free is enough if:

  • You want sounds now and then, or just want to hear a switch. The browser tester above does that.
  • You also use Windows or Linux. KeyEcho, Mechvibes and Keyboard Sounds Pro run on all three.
  • You want to read or change the code. Mechvibes, KeyEcho, the open-source Thock and Keyboard Sounds Pro are open source.
  • You have a MacBook with the motion sensor and don't mind a Terminal step. Clank Keys gives you force-sensitive sound for nothing.

Keyslam is worth $3.99 if:

  • You want force-sensitive sound in an app Apple has notarized, with no Terminal step. On MacBooks with the motion sensor, Keyslam reads the accelerometer about 800 times a second and picks real strikes from four strength bands.
  • You want named boards with documented sources: Cherry MX Blue, Cherry MX Brown, Cherry MX Red, Cherry MX Black (Leopold FC980M PD), Gateron Red (GMMK TKL), Topre, Leopold FC660M with MX Browns, WhiteFox (Hako Violet), Rainy 75, Unicomp buckling spring, IBM Model F XT, vintage Technics, MacBook Pro (2018) keyboard, IBM Selectric II typewriter and Olivetti Lettera 22 typewriter. Each is cut from CC0 Freesound recordings, credited to the people who made them.
  • You're on an Intel Mac, a desktop or macOS 13. Keyslam runs on all of them and plays a strength you pick where there's no sensor.
  • You want an app that makes no network connections, and pauses on its own while any app is using the microphone.

Free keyboard sound app questions

Is there a completely free keyboard sound app for Mac?

Yes. Mechvibes, Thock (the open-source one), Keyboard Sounds Pro and Clank Keys charge nothing for anything, and Tacque asks only for an optional $1.99 tip. KeyEcho, KeyClicker and Kachunk are free with paid extras. Mechvibes runs only on Apple silicon for now, and Clank Keys isn't notarized.

Is Mechvibes completely free on a Mac?

Yes. The app is free and MIT-licensed, and every pack is free too. On a Mac, version 3.4.0 is an experimental build for Apple silicon only and isn't notarized, so macOS blocks the first launch until you allow it, and you re-add its permissions after each update.

Is Keyslam free?

No. Keyslam is free for 7 days with everything unlocked and no account or card, then $3.99 once for a lifetime license. The browser keyboard simulator is free with no time limit.

Is Keyboard Sounds Pro safe?

We haven't audited it, so we can't vouch for it. What we can check: it's MIT-licensed with its code on GitHub, and the Mac installer comes from the project's GitHub releases. Its pages don't say whether the Mac build is notarized or what it sends online. Like any keyboard sound app, it needs your permission to see key presses, so download it only from GitHub and read what Input Monitoring lets an app see before you allow it.

Which free keyboard sound app works on Intel Macs?

KeyEcho and Keyboard Sounds Pro have Intel builds. Mechvibes 3 doesn't yet (an Intel build is merged for its next release), and Clank Keys is Apple silicon only. Keyslam runs on Intel Macs with macOS 13 or later, free for 7 days. More in keyboard sounds on Intel Macs and desktops.
