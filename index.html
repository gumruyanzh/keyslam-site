Force sensing

Your MacBook already feels how hard you type.

MacBooks with an M1 Pro, M1 Max, M2 or later chip have a motion sensor inside the case. Keyslam reads it about 800 times a second and compares each keystroke's jolt with how you usually type. Then it picks real strikes from that board's soft, medium, hard and slam bands and crossfades between the two closest.

The sound starts as soon as the key goes down, usually at your typical strength, and settles on the measured strength within about 16 ms. You hear one keystroke, not two. Read how these keyboard sounds that react to how hard you type are measured.

The recordings

Real strikes, sorted soft to slam

Every pack starts as one real typing recording, released as CC0 on Freesound; the two typewriters add a recorded bell for Return. Keyslam's slicer finds each strike and drops clipped or overlapping hits. On the keyboards it also pairs each strike with its key-up and keeps Space, Return and Delete apart (they ring longer). Then it sorts the strikes by natural loudness into four bands.

BandStrikes it takes from the recordingLevel target vs. mediumWhere it sits on a MacBook
Soft8th to 30th percentile0.55×about 0.4× your usual keystroke
Medium38th to 62nd percentile1× (the reference)your usual keystroke
Hard70th to 88th percentile1.45×about 2.2×
Slam92nd to 100th percentile, the hardest ~8%2×about 4× and up

Slam isn't a separate recording. It's the hardest ~8% of strikes from someone typing normally. Strikes that fall between two bands are skipped, so neighboring bands sound clearly different. Each band is then nudged toward its own level target, and every pack is matched to the same loudness (-28 LUFS), so switching boards doesn't jump in volume.

Privacy

An app with no network connections, no analytics and no account.

Hears keys, not words

Input Monitoring tells Keyslam which key went down. It never stores or sends what you type, and macOS keeps password fields hidden from all apps.

Checks your license offline

Your key is Ed25519-signed and verified on your Mac. The app never contacts a server, not even to check the license.

Trial date in your Keychain

The trial start date and your license live in your Mac's Keychain. There's no account to make.

Buy in your browser

Checkout runs on Stripe's page in your browser. The app only opens the link.

Small, native and light on your Mac

About 16 MB

That's the whole app, all 15 packs included. Native Swift, no Electron.

A 5 ms audio buffer

A C audio core holds every take in memory with an audio buffer of about 5 ms, so a key press only starts a sound.

Every key its own

Each key keeps its own take and pitch, like a real board, so fast typing doesn't sound like one sample on repeat.

Stereo by position

Q sits a little left and P a little right, like the keyboard in front of you. One checkbox turns it off.

Key-up sounds

Real keys sound on the way back up too. On the keyboard packs, Keyslam plays a recorded release, quieter after a soft press. The typewriter packs have no key-up sound.

Rests when you do

After 3 minutes without typing, the audio and the motion sensor switch off until your next key press.

Bring your own pack

Drop WAV, AIFF or FLAC recordings with a small pack.json into the Packs folder from the menu bar.

Open at login

Tick one box and the sounds are there after every restart, from the menu bar.

Questions people ask first

Does the Keyslam app connect to the internet?

No. The app makes no network connections at all. Your license key is Ed25519-signed and checked offline on your Mac, and the trial start date lives in your Mac's Keychain. You only go online in your browser, to download Keyslam or to pay on Stripe's checkout page.

Is Keyslam a native Mac app?

Yes. It's written in Swift with a C real-time audio core, not Electron or a web wrapper. The whole app, with all 15 packs, is about 16 MB, the audio buffer is about 5 ms, and after 3 idle minutes it switches its audio and the motion sensor off.

Which apps does Keyslam play sounds in?

Any app you type in. It listens to the keyboard system-wide, with the Input Monitoring permission, instead of inside one app. Password fields are the exception: macOS hides those keystrokes from all apps, so nothing plays there.

Can I try Keyslam before I buy it?

Yes. The Mac app is free for 7 days with everything unlocked, and the board on this page plays the same 15 recordings in your browser.
