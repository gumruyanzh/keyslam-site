Mechanical keyboard sound effects on every keystroke

A good keyboard sound effect has to keep up with fast typing and never sound like the same sample on a loop. Here is what Keyslam does in the few milliseconds after you press a key:

  1. macOS reports the key. With the Input Monitoring permission, Keyslam learns only which key went down. It can't change or block what you type, and it never stores or sends it.
  2. It picks that key's take. Letters use one set of takes; on the keyboard packs, Space, Return and Delete share a separate set of darker, longer-ringing strikes. Every key keeps its own take and pitch, the way each key on a real board sounds slightly different.
  3. It picks the strength. On MacBooks with the motion sensor, Keyslam reads the accelerometer about 800 times a second, compares the jolt with how you usually type, and blends real strikes from the soft to slam bands. On other Macs you pick the strength.
  4. It places and plays the sound left or right by key position, through a C audio core with an audio buffer of about 5 ms, on your speakers or headphones.
  5. The key comes back up and the board's key-up sound plays; with force sensing on, it's softer after a soft press.

Mechanical keyboard sounds on Mac: questions

Is there a clicky keyboard sound app for Mac?

Yes. Keyslam is a clicky keyboard sound app for Mac when you pick the MX Blue board: every press plays a real Cherry MX Blue click, and the key-up click plays as the key returns. For the buckling-spring click of an IBM-style board, pick Unicomp or Model F. Pick MX Brown or Topre if you want the mechanical sound without the click.

Is it just a fake mechanical keyboard sound?

It's a fake mechanical keyboard sound in one sense: your keyboard stays the same, and the sound comes from your speakers or headphones. But nothing is synthesized. Each of the 15 boards is cut from a real recording of that keyboard or typewriter, credited to the person who made it.

How do I get mechanical keyboard sounds on my Mac?

Install Keyslam (step-by-step in how to make your MacBook sound like a mechanical keyboard), allow Input Monitoring when macOS asks, and pick a board from the menu bar. Every key you press then plays that board, in any app, until you switch it off from the same menu.

Can I use it with headphones or AirPods?

Yes. Sound follows your current output, including when you switch to headphones or AirPods. With headphones on, people on a call usually won't hear it. Bluetooth adds its own lag: why AirPods sound late, and how to test it.

Will it slow down my Mac?

It's built to stay light. Every recording is already in memory, so a key press only starts a sound, and the audio and motion sensor switch off after 3 minutes without typing. In our self-test on an M5 Pro MacBook Pro, the audio engine, key tap, one loaded pack and the motion sensor together peaked at about 11 MB of memory. The app is about 16 MB installed.
