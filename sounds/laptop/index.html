The butterfly keyboard

Apple introduced the butterfly mechanism in the 12-inch MacBook in 2015 and used it in MacBook Pro and MacBook Air models until 2019 and 2020, when it went back to scissor switches under the name Magic Keyboard. Butterfly keys move far less than a mechanical switch, so there's no long travel to make noise, just the cap tapping down onto the frame.

The 2018 MacBook Pro has the third generation, which Apple described as quieter than earlier butterfly keyboards; a thin membrane sits under each key. That is what you hear here: a short, damped tap. For a membrane-vs-mechanical A/B, the keyboard sound test page compares this pack with MX Brown.

What this recording adds

The uploader recorded “various typings” close up: multiple keystrokes and single clicks at different speeds, 53 seconds as a 24-bit, 48 kHz mono WAV. A shotgun microphone this close turns a quiet keyboard into a clear, usable sound. Keyslam kept six regular-key takes per band, 2–3 per band for Space, Return and Delete, and 5 key-up takes.

Because the source is mono, the stereo spread in the app comes from Keyslam placing each key by its position.

Who it suits

Pick Laptop for shared rooms and late nights (with the volume down), or if you want gentle confirmation of each keystroke without pretending to have a different keyboard.

Pick something else if you want your MacBook to sound mechanical: MX Brown for a classic tactile, Topre for something soft but deep.

MacBook butterfly keyboard: questions

Is this the sound of a current MacBook keyboard?

No, it's the 2018 butterfly keyboard. Apple silicon MacBooks use scissor keys (Apple's Magic Keyboard), which travel further, so they sound different. Keyslam doesn't have a scissor-key pack.

Was this recorded with the MacBook's own microphone?

No. khenshom used an external Sennheiser MKE600 shotgun microphone on a Zoom H5 recorder, close up, per the Freesound page.

Why add a laptop keyboard sound to a laptop?

Built-in keys are quiet, and some people type more confidently with audible feedback. This pack adds it without sounding like a different keyboard. In the app it plays at the same loudness as every other pack, so set the volume low if you want it subtle.
