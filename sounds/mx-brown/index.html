How a Brown makes its sound

The Brown is Cherry's tactile switch. The legs of its stem have a small bump that pushes past the metal contact leaf about 2 mm down, so you feel the moment the key registers. There is no click jacket, so that bump is nearly silent.

That leaves two sounds: the stem hitting the bottom of the housing and the stem returning to the top. The tactile peak is around 55 g. Many people treat Browns as the middle ground between a clicky Blue and a smooth Red, and they're a common pick for office and programming boards.

What this recording adds

The file is a 16-bit, 48 kHz stereo FLAC from August 2017, just over a minute long. The uploader describes it in one line and doesn't name the keyboard or keycaps. Even so, a minute of steady typing gave the slicer enough clean strikes to fill every band with six regular-key takes, plus 2–4 takes per band for Space, Return and Delete.

The same switch appears in our Leopold pack, recorded on a different board with different keycaps and a microphone between the hands. Together they are a useful A/B of how much the keyboard, not the switch, shapes the sound.

Who it suits

Pick MX Brown for writing, coding and office typing, or if Blues feel too sharp and Reds too plain.

Pick something else if you want more detail in each keystroke (Leopold) or a softer, deeper sound (Topre).

Cherry MX Brown: questions

How quiet is the MX Brown pack next to MX Blue?

In Keyslam they play at the same loudness: every pack is leveled so a standard medium-strength typing passage measures −28 LUFS. The difference is tone. Brown has no high click, so it sounds less sharp at the same volume. Turn the volume down if you need it quieter.

Why does MX Brown sound different on the Leopold page?

Same switch, different keyboard, keycaps and microphone. The Leopold recording has enjoyPBT and GMK keycaps and a recorder on a wrist rest right in front of the space bar, and its uploader calls it the “more trebley” of their two takes.

Which keyboard and microphone were used for this MX Brown recording?

The uploader doesn't name either. The Freesound page is tagged “zoom” and “h1”, which suggests a Zoom H1 recorder, but that's a reading of the tags, not a stated fact. The switch is the one thing the description confirms.
